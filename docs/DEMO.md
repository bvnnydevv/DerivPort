# DerivPort: two-minute demo

Before presenting, set **Data range** to **Frozen demo (2018–2024)**. Use the original five-stock comparison (**AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, AMZN, GOOGL**), select **AAPL** and **May 3, 2024**, and leave the next 21 common sessions hidden. Keep `predicted_return - k * uncertainty` with `k = 2`. This stock and date were curated for an understandable earnings example, not selected as proof that the model succeeds. The numerical examples below apply only to this frozen comparison, not to the default full-history mode or a new universe.

**0:00–0:15 — Set the experiment.** “We are back at the market close on May 3, 2024. We have five stocks and only information available then. Our question is what this evidence suggested about Apple over the next 21 sessions.” Point out the hidden outcome and the shared stock/date above both analyst views. Mention that the search also supports international listings and that the calendar accepts many historical dates.

**0:15–0:45 — Read the two views.** Apple's May 2 release reported record Services revenue alongside a 4% decline in total revenue. Read the separate interpretation and limitation. The model estimates **+2.51%**, with **8.77 percentage points** of typical forecast error, placing Apple **3rd of 5** by predicted return. “Company evidence describes a business; a ranking compares five stocks. These views can emphasize different things. The company note has made no numerical forecast, so we do not assign it prediction accuracy.”

**0:45–1:15 — Change one mathematical assumption.** In the algorithm builder, show the LaTeX expression for `predicted_return - k * uncertainty`: at `k = 2`, Apple's score is **−15.03**, placing it **2nd** under the student rule. In the calculus lab, vary uncertainty by one percentage point: its derivative is **−2**, curvature is **0**, and both the tangent estimate and integrated derivative give the same **−2-point** score change. “A score changes rank only when it crosses another stock's score.” Set `k` to **0.20**, then **0.25**: Apple and Alphabet cross at approximately **0.201**. Apple's rank moves from **3rd to 2nd**, although its score falls. Return `k` to **2**.

**1:15–1:35 — Test across past dates.** Run a backtest from **January 1 through December 31, 2023**, using the same comparison and the current rule. Point to the number of completed test periods, the error comparison, and the separate ranking results. “Every forecast used evidence available at its own cutoff. The algorithms share the same periods. Choosing a rule after exploring these results can overfit; this is a research comparison, not untouched proof.” Keep these past-period results separate from the still-hidden May 2024 replay.

**1:35–2:00 — Reveal what happened.** Return to the May 3 replay and click the reveal button. Apple actually returned **+6.13%**, finishing **2nd**. Explain: “Spearman correlation measures how closely the ranked orders agree: 1 is the same order and −1 is the reverse.” On this date, the AI model's correlation is **+0.80**, momentum's is **+0.10**, and the original student rule's is **−0.30**. Change `k` once to show the **edited after seeing the outcome** marker. Finish: “Our score was a hypothesis. The outcome gives us evidence to investigate, not a buy or sell recommendation.”

After the main demo, search **Toyota** or **7203.T** and compare it with another cached listing. Where no dated company note is available, the app should say so; do not apply Apple's company explanation to a different listing. International comparisons use common observed dates and local-currency adjusted returns, without currency conversion. Loading a new uncached listing or searching the online provider needs internet access; cached listings remain available offline.

To explain the expanded dates, choose **Full cached history** and a **2026** cutoff. The supplied histories run through **November 13, 2026**. Select the latest observed close to show that research still works while the next 21-session outcome is pending; the reveal button stays disabled until those actual observations exist in the cache. This makes the difference between available evidence and a completed historical experiment explicit.

For **2037**, open **Future scenarios** and choose **December 31, 2037**. Point to **SIMULATED**, the real starting observation, and the annual log-drift, volatility, and correlation assumptions. “We do not have stock prices from 2037. These monthly paths show what our stated assumptions can produce; their bands are simulation percentiles, not known outcomes or validated forecasts.” Change one assumption and compare the possibilities. Keep these scenarios separate from historical backtest results and company evidence.

For a quick backtest consistency check, set the formula to `momentum`: the student rule should exactly match the momentum baseline's selections and results. For the simulation demonstration, show rally, sudden reversal, and sideways/volatile scenarios. These projections use shared reproducible paths under stated assumptions, and stay separate from historical results.

For a deeper calculus demonstration, load **DerivPort Signal** and inspect its partial derivatives: model estimate **0.6**, momentum **0.4**, uncertainty **−0.5k**, and volatility **−0.25k**. Change the selected variable and perturbation to compare the tangent, quadratic approximation, and integrated slope. These values describe the explicit ranking formula; do not present them as measured feature influence in the regression or as forecasts of stock-price movement. For a nonlinear custom expression, show how curvature changes the gap between tangent approximation and actual score change, and how an undefined interval is rejected.
